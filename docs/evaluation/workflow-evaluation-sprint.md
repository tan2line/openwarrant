# Workflow Evaluation Sprint

**Know what your AI workflow can support, where it fails, and what decision the evidence justifies.**

Proposed founding offer · For Andrew's review · Pricing is a planning hypothesis, not a market benchmark or a commitment by any partner.

## The buyer and the problem

The first buyer is a healthcare AI program lead or clinical operations leader with one bounded AI-assisted workflow approaching a pilot or a material change. The buyer needs a defensible evidence packet for a specific decision: proceed under conditions, fix identified problems, collect more evidence, or pause.

Common entry points include a clinical review packet assistant, a record-summarization workflow, or a policy-grounded internal assistant. Start with one workflow, a named decision owner and clear human review boundaries.

## The promise

In a proposed ten-business-day sprint after scope and inputs are ready, establish the evaluation protocol, assess a baseline and candidate configuration, document material failures and reviewer disagreements, and deliver a decision-ready findings packet with a retest plan.

The engagement delivers evidence and analysis. The customer's authorized decision-maker retains the deployment decision. A synthetic demonstration is not a substitute for clinical validation.

## Founding scope

- One workflow and one defined intended-user group.
- One agreed use boundary, dependency inventory and decision question.
- One versioned rubric with explicit critical failures and escalation criteria.
- Up to 25 initial scenarios for protocol development and demonstration; validation coverage and sample size are separately agreed before any readiness claim.
- One baseline and one candidate configuration, with repeated-trial design agreed if actual model variability is assessed.
- Two qualified customer-designated reviewers for independent assessments; a designated adjudicator for disputes.
- One structured findings readout, evidence export and remediation/retest plan.
- One bounded retest of corrected findings within the agreed scope.

## What the customer receives

| Deliverable | Decision value |
|---|---|
| Workflow boundary and dependency map | Makes intended use and reserved human decisions explicit |
| Versioned evaluation protocol | Documents what success, failure and insufficient evidence mean |
| Scenario inventory and coverage statement | Shows tested failure modes and what remains untested |
| Baseline/candidate comparison | Exposes regressions even when aggregate results improve |
| Reviewer and adjudication record | Preserves uncertainty and the reasons for resolving disagreement |
| Evidence receipt | Binds findings to versions, limitations and review triggers |
| Decision brief and retest plan | Gives the accountable owner concrete next actions |

## Proposed schedule

| Window | Work and exit condition |
|---|---|
| Days 1–2 | Confirm buyer's decision, intended use, access, policy sources and reviewer availability; agree protocol |
| Days 3–4 | Build and inspect scenarios; agree rubric and protected validation approach where needed |
| Days 5–6 | Run the configurations; capture outputs/traces and automatic findings |
| Days 7–8 | Independent expert review, calibration and adjudication |
| Days 9–10 | Readout, evidence packet, restrictions and remediation plan |

The schedule begins after required inputs and reviewer time are available. Delayed access or scope changes require replanning. Clinical outcome studies, comprehensive legal review and institutional validation are separately scoped.

## Proposed pricing to test

**Founding sprint: $7,500 fixed fee**, with customer-provided domain reviewers and adjudicator. This is a proposed starting price to test in buyer conversations. Expert recruitment, protected-data infrastructure, substantial integrations, extensive scenario authoring and clinical validation studies are separately estimated.

Working economics: 30–40 delivery hours produces roughly $188–$250 gross revenue per delivery hour before overhead, expenses and taxes. This is arithmetic from the proposed fee, not a margin forecast. Scope overruns and reviewer coordination can substantially reduce the economics.

**Follow-on retest: from $2,500 per bounded change**, subject to the number of affected cases, configurations and review requirements. Offer recurring monitoring only after a paid pilot demonstrates repeat demand and defines what is actually being monitored.

Keep the first sale focused on the sprint. Treat the public starter as a demonstration and source of buyer conversations. Organization-specific evidence, expert interpretation and integration are the paid work.

## Intake questions

1. What decision must be made, by whom, and by when?
2. What is the assistant allowed to do, and what remains a human decision?
3. Which failures would change that decision, even if rare?
4. Which model, prompt, tools, source versions and baseline are available?
5. What evidence can be used, and under which access and handling arrangements?
6. Who can independently review the outputs and adjudicate disagreements?
7. What has changed since the last assessment?
8. What claims do you expect the results to support?

Your team has an AI workflow approaching a decision. We help define the tests that matter, examine where it fails, resolve expert disagreements and connect the findings to a clear use boundary. You receive a versioned evidence packet, a practical remediation plan and explicit triggers for reassessment.
